# Preterm Infant Feeding Guidelines (Post-Discharge)

## Why Preterm Babies Need Special Feeding

Babies born before 37 weeks of gestation have higher caloric and nutrient demands than full-term infants. Their bodies are still completing growth that would normally occur in the womb, including brain development, bone mineralization, and fat deposition. Standard breast milk or formula alone (20 kcal/oz) may not provide sufficient calories, protein, calcium, or phosphorus to support adequate catch-up growth. This is why many NICU-discharged preterm infants require fortified feeds for weeks to months after going home.

## Standard Fortification: 24 kcal/oz Breast Milk

The most common fortification prescribed at NICU discharge is 24 kilocalories per ounce (24 kcal/oz). This is achieved by adding a human milk fortifier (HMF) powder to expressed breast milk.

### Preparation Steps
1. Express breast milk into a clean, sterilized bottle.
2. Add one packet of powdered HMF (such as Similac Human Milk Fortifier or Enfamil HMF) to 25 mL of expressed breast milk.
3. Swirl the bottle gently in circular motions to dissolve. Do NOT shake vigorously, as shaking destroys protective immunoglobulins and creates air bubbles that increase gas and colic.
4. Use the fortified milk within 24 hours if stored in a refrigerator at 2–4 degrees Celsius.
5. Discard any fortified milk that has been at room temperature for more than 1 hour.
6. NEVER microwave breast milk or fortified breast milk. Microwaving creates dangerous hot spots that can scald the baby's mouth and throat, and it destroys beneficial antibodies.

### When the Pediatrician May Prescribe 22 kcal/oz Instead
If the baby shows signs of feeding intolerance — such as frequent spit-ups, abdominal distension (a visibly swollen or tight belly), or excessive gas — the pediatrician may temporarily step down from 24 kcal/oz to 22 kcal/oz to improve tolerance. Parents must NEVER change the fortification ratio on their own without explicit instruction from the baby's neonatologist or pediatrician.

## Formula Feeding for Preterm Infants

If breast milk is unavailable or insufficient, the pediatrician may prescribe a preterm transitional formula. These formulas are specifically designed with higher protein, calcium, phosphorus, and caloric density than standard infant formulas.

### Common Preterm Transitional Formulas
- Similac NeoSure (22 kcal/oz): For preterm infants from NICU discharge through approximately 12 months corrected age.
- Enfamil EnfaCare (22 kcal/oz): Similar nutritional profile to NeoSure, designed for catch-up growth.
- These are NOT the same as standard Similac Advance or Enfamil Infant. Standard formulas lack the additional nutrients preterm infants require.

### Preparation of Powdered Formula
1. Wash hands thoroughly with soap and water for at least 20 seconds.
2. Use boiled water that has been cooled to no less than 70 degrees Celsius (158 degrees Fahrenheit) for reconstitution.
3. Follow the exact scoop-to-water ratio printed on the formula can. Do NOT add extra water (dilutes nutrition dangerously) or extra powder (causes dehydration and kidney stress).
4. Prepared formula can be stored in the refrigerator for up to 24 hours. Discard after 24 hours.
5. Once a feeding has begun, discard any remaining formula in the bottle after 1 hour.

## Daily Volume Targets

The target daily intake for a preterm infant is 150 to 180 milliliters per kilogram of body weight per day (150–180 mL/kg/day).

### Calculating Target Volume
- Weigh the baby in kilograms (1 kg = 1000 grams).
- Multiply the weight by 150 mL (minimum) and 180 mL (maximum).
- Divide the total by the number of feeds per day (typically 8 to 10 feeds, every 2.5 to 3 hours).

### Example Calculation
- Baby weight: 2.0 kg (2000 grams)
- Minimum daily intake: 2.0 kg × 150 mL/kg = 300 mL per day
- Maximum daily intake: 2.0 kg × 180 mL/kg = 360 mL per day
- With 8 feeds per day: each feed = 37.5 mL to 45 mL
- With 10 feeds per day: each feed = 30 mL to 36 mL

### Signs of Adequate Intake
- Baby produces at least 6 to 8 wet diapers per day.
- Baby gains approximately 15 to 30 grams per day (measured at weekly weigh-ins, not daily).
- Baby is alert during wakeful periods and settles after feeds.

## Feeding Position and Reflux Prevention

Many preterm infants have gastroesophageal reflux (GER) due to an immature lower esophageal sphincter. To reduce reflux and spit-ups:

1. Hold the baby in an upright or semi-upright position (at least 30 degrees) during the entire feed.
2. After the feed is complete, keep the baby upright for 20 to 30 minutes before laying them down.
3. Burp the baby gently every 10 to 15 mL during the feed, not just at the end.
4. If using a bottle, choose a slow-flow or preemie nipple (such as Dr. Brown's Preemie Nipple) to prevent the baby from gulping air.
5. Do NOT overfeed. If the baby turns away, closes their mouth, or falls asleep, stop the feed. Forcing additional volume increases vomiting risk.

## Breastfeeding the Preterm Infant

Direct breastfeeding is encouraged and supported for preterm infants, but it may take additional time and practice compared to full-term babies. Preterm infants may have a weaker suck, tire more quickly, and require supplemental bottle feeds after nursing.

### Tips for Successful Breastfeeding
- Practice skin-to-skin contact (Kangaroo Mother Care) before and during breastfeeding sessions to promote letdown and bonding.
- Use a supplemental nursing system (SNS) if the baby needs fortified milk but the mother wishes to breastfeed directly.
- Pump after each breastfeeding session to maintain and build milk supply.
- A lactation consultant experienced with preterm infants can provide personalized positioning and latch guidance.

## Vitamin and Iron Supplementation

Most NICU-discharged preterm infants require the following daily supplements, prescribed by their pediatrician:

### Vitamin D
- Dose: 400 IU (International Units) per day, starting from discharge.
- Given as liquid drops (such as D-Vi-Sol or generic cholecalciferol drops).
- Administered once daily, ideally at the same time each day.
- Continue until the infant is consuming at least 1000 mL (approximately 32 ounces) of Vitamin D-fortified formula per day, which typically occurs around 6 to 12 months.

### Iron
- Dose: 2 mg/kg/day of elemental iron for preterm infants.
- Given as liquid ferrous sulfate drops (such as Fer-In-Sol) or as part of a multivitamin with iron (such as Poly-Vi-Sol with Iron).
- Begin at 2 to 4 weeks of age or at hospital discharge, whichever is later.
- Administer between feeds for best absorption. Iron may cause dark-colored stools; this is normal and expected.
- Continue iron supplementation through at least 12 months of age or until the infant is eating sufficient iron-rich solid foods, as directed by the pediatrician.

## When to Contact the Pediatrician About Feeding

Contact the baby's pediatrician or neonatologist if any of the following occur:
- The baby consistently refuses feeds or takes less than 50 percent of the prescribed volume for more than 2 consecutive feeds.
- The baby vomits forcefully (projectile vomiting) after most feeds.
- The vomit contains green (bilious) material or blood.
- The baby has fewer than 4 wet diapers in a 24-hour period.
- The baby is not gaining weight or is losing weight at the weekly weigh-in.
- The baby appears excessively sleepy and difficult to wake for feeds.
