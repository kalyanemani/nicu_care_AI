# Kangaroo Mother Care (KMC) for Low Birth Weight and Preterm Infants

## What Is Kangaroo Mother Care

Kangaroo Mother Care (KMC) is the practice of holding an infant in direct skin-to-skin contact against the parent's bare chest, in an upright position, secured with a wrap or binder. The baby wears only a diaper and a cap. The parent's body heat maintains the baby's temperature, the baby hears the parent's heartbeat, and the close contact promotes breastfeeding and bonding.

KMC was originally developed in Colombia in 1978 as an alternative to incubator care for stable preterm infants, and has since been endorsed by the World Health Organization as a standard of care for all newborns, with particular benefit for low birth weight (less than 2500 grams) and preterm infants.

## Benefits of Kangaroo Mother Care

### For the Baby
- Thermal regulation: The parent's body automatically adjusts temperature to warm a cold baby and cool an overheated baby. This is more effective and more stable than incubator heating for stable infants.
- Improved weight gain: Studies show that infants receiving regular KMC gain weight faster than those in standard crib care, partly due to reduced caloric expenditure on thermoregulation.
- More stable heart rate and breathing: Skin-to-skin contact reduces episodes of apnea (breathing pauses) and bradycardia (slow heart rate) in preterm infants.
- Better sleep quality: Infants in KMC spend more time in quiet, restorative sleep and less time in agitated states.
- Enhanced breastfeeding: The close proximity to the breast encourages the baby to root and suckle, promoting earlier and more successful direct breastfeeding.
- Reduced risk of serious infections: KMC has been shown to reduce the risk of neonatal sepsis and nosocomial (hospital-acquired) infections.

### For the Mother
- Increased breast milk production: Skin-to-skin contact stimulates oxytocin and prolactin release, increasing both milk volume and letdown efficiency.
- Reduced postpartum depression and anxiety: Mothers practicing KMC report lower levels of stress and higher feelings of confidence and bonding.
- Empowerment and confidence: Mothers feel more competent and connected to their baby's care, especially after the NICU experience where they may have felt helpless.

### For the Father and Other Caregivers
- Fathers, partners, and grandparents can also practice KMC. The thermal and bonding benefits apply to any adult caregiver.

## How to Practice KMC at Home After NICU Discharge

### Preparation
1. Choose a comfortable chair or recliner in a quiet, warm room (room temperature 22 to 24 degrees Celsius or 72 to 75 degrees Fahrenheit).
2. Remove the baby's clothing except for a clean diaper and a cap or beanie (babies lose significant heat from their heads).
3. The parent should wear a loose, open-front shirt or no shirt. A zip-up hoodie worn backwards works well to secure the baby against the chest.

### Positioning
1. Hold the baby upright against the bare chest, between the breasts (or on the chest for fathers).
2. The baby's head should be turned to one side, with the face visible and the airway clear at all times.
3. The baby's legs should be flexed in a frog-like position (hips bent, knees drawn up). This is the natural, comfortable position for a newborn.
4. Secure the baby with a stretchy wrap, cloth binder, or KMC band so both of the parent's hands are free. The wrap should be snug enough that the baby does not slide but not so tight that it restricts the baby's breathing or chest expansion.

### Safety Checks During KMC
- Ensure the baby's face is always visible and not buried into the parent's chest or clothing. The nostrils must remain unobstructed.
- The baby's chin should be slightly extended (tilted up), not flexed toward the chest. A chin-to-chest position can obstruct the airway.
- Monitor the baby's skin color: it should be pink or appropriate for the baby's ethnicity. Blue or pale discoloration around the lips, fingernails, or toes requires immediate medical attention.
- If the parent feels drowsy, transfer the baby to a safe crib or bassinet before falling asleep. KMC must only be performed while the parent is awake and alert.

## Optimal Timing and When to Practice KMC

### Best Time and Daily Schedule
- **Between Feeds:** The best time to practice KMC is between feeds or 30 to 45 minutes after feeding when the baby has burped and is settled. Avoid practicing KMC immediately after a heavy feed in a flat position to prevent gastroesophageal reflux or regurgitation.
- **Quiet Alertness and Sleep Transitions:** Ideal when the infant is in a quiet, alert state or transitioning into sleep. The warmth and parent's rhythmic heartbeat calm the baby into restorative quiet sleep.
- **Daily Frequency and Sessions:** Practice KMC 2 to 4 times a day, aiming for at least 60 continuous minutes per session. Morning, afternoon, and evening sessions can easily be integrated into the family's daily routine.
- **Fussiness or Thermal Instability:** KMC should also be initiated whenever the baby is fussy, crying, hypothermic, or adjusting after diaper changes or medical checkups, as skin-to-skin contact rapidly stabilizes neonatal body temperature, heart rate, and oxygen saturation.

## Duration and Frequency

### Recommended Duration
- Each KMC session should last at least 60 minutes to allow the baby to complete a full sleep cycle and derive maximum thermal and physiological benefit.
- Longer sessions (2 to 4 hours or more) are encouraged if the parent and baby are comfortable.
- Intermittent KMC (multiple sessions per day) is beneficial even if continuous KMC is not feasible.

### How Long to Continue
- KMC can be practiced for as long as the parent and baby find it comfortable and beneficial. Many families continue KMC well past the newborn period.
- Minimum recommended period: until the baby reaches full-term equivalent age (40 weeks post-menstrual age) or weighs at least 2500 grams and is maintaining temperature independently outside of KMC.

## KMC and Breastfeeding

Kangaroo care is an ideal time to attempt direct breastfeeding. The baby is calm, warm, and in close proximity to the breast.

### Tips
- Watch for early hunger cues during KMC: rooting (turning the head toward the breast), hand-to-mouth movements, lip-smacking, or restless wriggling.
- Offer the breast when these cues appear. Do not wait for crying, which is a late hunger cue and can make latching more difficult.
- If the baby is too sleepy or weak to breastfeed directly, express milk and offer it via a cup, syringe, or supplemental nursing system while maintaining skin-to-skin contact.

## When NOT to Practice KMC

KMC is contraindicated or requires medical guidance in the following situations:
- The baby is on mechanical ventilation or requires continuous positive airway pressure (CPAP). Discuss with the NICU team whether KMC is feasible with respiratory support.
- The baby has an umbilical catheter, chest drain, or other invasive line in place. KMC may be possible with careful positioning, but only under nursing supervision.
- The parent has an active, contagious skin infection (such as herpes lesions on the chest). Cover the affected area or postpone KMC until the infection is treated.
- The baby is clinically unstable (active seizures, severe apnea, hemodynamic instability). Resume KMC once stabilized.
